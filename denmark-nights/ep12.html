PREVIEW — IN ENGLISH

Monday began with a new calendar.

Rita came in at five-thirty through the back, the way she had come in for six years, and she did the day in its order. Lights. Coffee on. Stools down, last stool first. Pie case: one chess, one coconut, half a chocolate, the weekend written in what was left of it. On the wall by the register the calendar had been turned in the night, and the new month lay there mostly empty — clean squares, a truck at the top of the page, nothing yet crossed, nothing yet lived in, except that she was standing in the first of it. The emptiness was not a promise. It was just paper that had not been written on yet. She had learned, this season, the exact weight of paper that had.

At the pass there was no slice to cut. Monday had never asked for one. She stood a moment with the knife drawer open anyway, out of a habit that had started keeping Thursdays on other days of the week, and then she shut the drawer and took down the temperature log and read the dial over the walk-in door: thirty-six, and holding. In the log, the evening line from the night before stood in her own small square hand. This week the lines were hers at both ends of the day, and the log did not comment on it, and neither did she. She wrote the morning line where the morning line went.

Behind the register, the counter book sat with its rubber band around it twice, and in the back of it lay a season of Thursdays with one gap in it that nobody had written anything on at all. She did not open it. Monday asked for coffee ahead of the cups and a grill warmed through and the front door unlocked at six, and she gave Monday all of that, in its order, and the room opened — ordinary, and lit, and hers to run.

The Monday cast was smaller than Thursday's and she ran it by heart. Old Harlan came in at seven with his newspaper and an opinion about the new month.

"New month's when a fellow finds out what last month decided," Harlan said, over his eggs. "Papers all settle in the new month. Jackson'll have its say now the calendar's turned."

"Jackson has its say every month I've known it," Rita said, and refilled him without being asked. "Eat your eggs while it's still talking, Harlan."

"Eggs is best eaten ahead of all sayings," Harlan said, and allowed the sports page to console him, and the morning went on — whole, and ordinary, and lit, with a gap standing in a book behind the register that nobody in the room said anything about at all.

That morning, in Jackson, in an office off the bypass, a man in his other shirt sat down across a desk from the man who had watched him sign his name three times, and this time the paper on the desk was his.

He had not brought it in a company envelope. It was folded once, in the inside pocket of the shirt, and he took it out and unfolded it and laid it flat on the desk where the acceptance had lain, and smoothed it once with the side of his hand, the way he left every place he worked — straight, and square, and readable.

It was one page, in his square hand, and it said what it said in the order a working man would say it if saying were his trade, which it was not, and the page knew it:

I decline the regional supervisor appointment, effective before assumption of duties. I ask that my acceptance of Thursday last be rescinded and this letter filed in its place. — E. Brandt

Under it, clipped at the corner the way another stack had been clipped, were two more pages: a sheet of gauge rows in square capitals, a season of them, ending in a gap, and a request, in the same hand, in form's own grammar — corrected condition data for one asset, and a petition for the final verification already pending on its order to be held before any scheduling, the asset to stand or fall on the verification and on nothing else.
