ABOUT THIS EPISODE

마지막 목요일 아침, Rita는 시즌 내내 잘라 두던 체스 파이 한 조각을 자르지 않고 뒷선반은 하루 종일 비어 있다. 밤 점검 뒤 그녀는 조각을 새로 잘라 패스가 아닌 카운터, 파이 진열장 옆 의자에 놓고, Ellis는 6년 만에 처음으로 의자에 앉아 앉은 채로 저녁을 먹는다. 마감 뒤에는 Rita도 자기 이름이 적힌 주문표로 자기 카운터에 앉고, 다섯 달 뒤 금요일 — 그는 처음으로 정문으로 들어온다.

PREVIEW — IN ENGLISH

The back shelf stood bare on Thursday morning, and that was the day entire.

Rita came in at five-thirty through the back, the way she had come in for six years, and she did the day in its order. Lights. Coffee on. Stools down, last stool first. Pie case: one chess, one coconut, half a chocolate. The dial over the walk-in door said thirty-six, and holding, and underneath the morning's ordinary sounds the box held its new note, low and even, a box keeping its cold without working for it. She wrote the morning line in the temperature log, in her small square hand, under the line in the square hand that was not hers, and put her name tag on over her heart, and counted the drawer.

At the pass she took down a plate and a square of wrap, the way her hands had done it every Thursday of the season before the rest of her was awake, and she opened the pie case and stood with the chess pie in front of her and her knife in her hand.

Then she put the knife down.

She closed the case on the whole pie. She set the plate back on its stack and the wrap back in its box, and she left the back shelf the way the night had left it, which was empty, and went out to open the room at six, and all day long, behind everything the room did, the shelf stood bare in the kitchen like a word nobody had said yet.

The Thursday cast came in on their clocks, and the room was still the room.

The produce man came in at a quarter past six with his cap cold from the lot. Thursday was his double day — Memphis in the morning empty and back through by dark full — and he took his counter stool and ate the big breakfast there like a citizen, and paid standing up, the way he paid on his double day, and was gone into the morning.

The tanker man out of Memphis took the last stool without looking at it. Thursday was his far point — he turned around tonight and slept in his own bed tomorrow — and his company had kept the late turn, so he would roll at ten again, which meant two found hours of Thursday to spend somewhere with coffee in it, and he settled to spending them at the speed of a man in no hurry at all.

Old Harlan came in at seven with his newspaper and his opinions and wanted his eggs drowned, and ate them, and delivered himself of the view that a fellow could hardly hear himself think for the quiet of that box.

"A quiet box is a box doing its work, Harlan," Rita said, and refilled him without being asked. "Eat your eggs."

The woman driver who came through on Thursdays only, hauling flowers to Memphis, took her stool and Rita cut her chess pie for breakfast from the whole pie in the case, the way she cut any customer's pie, at the moment it was asked for, and set it down in front of her, and the woman ate it unashamed, the road owing her one sweet thing a week and her collecting it. "One of these weeks I'll have the eggs like a citizen," she said.

"One of these weeks," Rita said, and the morning went on, whole and ordinary and lit, and in the kitchen the back shelf stood bare, and nobody in the dining room knew a thing about it, and the two people in the kitchen knew it the way you know weather in the next room.

Merle knew it first. He stood at the pass a moment with his coffee and looked at the shelf where a covered plate had waited every Thursday of the season, and he looked at Rita, and he said nothing at all, which for Merle was a speech of some length, and went to his grill.

Dee came on at two with her school envelope in her apron, and in the first lull she came back through the kitchen past the pass, and her eyes went to the back shelf the way eyes in this building went to anything that was not where it belonged. She looked at the bare shelf. She looked at Rita. She took the coffee pot out of her hand in passing and went out to the room, and she did not ask, and the not-asking was its own kind of fluency, learned in this room, off a pad page with one word on it.
